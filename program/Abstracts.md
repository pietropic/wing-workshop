**Siarhei Finski** *Blackboard*

*title:* The metric entropy of spaces of holomorphic functions

*abstract:* How much information is needed to determine a function up to a given accuracy? Pontryagin-Schnirelmann and Kolmogorov made this question precise with the notion of metric entropy: the logarithm of the minimal number of balls of a fixed radius needed to cover a compact subset of a metric space. This talk is devoted to a problem posed by Kolmogorov: computing the metric entropy of spaces of holomorphic functions.

More precisely, for a compact subset of a complex manifold, consider the continuous functions on it that extend holomorphically to the whole manifold with a uniform bound. By Montel’s theorem, this family is compact in the topology of uniform convergence. Our aim is to make this compactness quantitative by determining the asymptotic behavior of its metric entropy. For domains in a complex vector space, this was determined through the work of Vitushkin, Zakharyuta, Nivoche and Bandtlow. I will explain a new proof that extends this result to arbitrary complex manifolds, drawing on algebraic geometry, approximation theory, and pluripotential theory.

**Yifan Chen** *Blackboard*

*title:* Kähler surfaces with semipositive anticanonical bundle

*abstract:* I will discuss recent work on the classification of compact Kähler surfaces with semipositive anticanonical bundle. In particular, we prove that the blowup of projective plane at any nine points on a smooth cubic is one of them, answering a question of Demailly–Peternell–Schneider. I will explain how cylindrical geometry on a punctured neighborhood of an anticanonical elliptic curve gives a pluriharmonic function with logarithmic growth, and hence the semipositive Hermitian metric on the anticanonical bundle. This is joint work with Simion Filip, Song Sun, Valentino Tosatti, and Junsheng Zhang.

**Bo Berndtsson** *slides*

*title:* Ehrhart's volume conjecture - reading AI-generated proofs.

*abstract:* Ehrhart's volume conjecture from 1967 was recently proved by Open AI and Antrophic in two separate articles. In the lecture I will state and motivate the conjecture, briefly discuss the proof(s), and conclude with some reflections on AI-generated articles versus texts written by humans. Time permitting, I will also show a funny picture.
